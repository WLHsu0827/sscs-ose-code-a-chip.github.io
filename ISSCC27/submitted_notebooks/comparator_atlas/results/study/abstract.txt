Comparator Atlas: When Calibration Is Not Enough
Wei-Lun Hsu - National Tsing Hua University

Comparator Atlas is an openly licensed notebook and offline explorer for reproducible finite-deadline characterization of SKY130 dynamic comparators. A declared family of nine standard/low-threshold and trim-bank designs is evaluated under fixed stimuli. Candidate selection uses three training conditions and explicit core-energy and gate-area-proxy budgets before the selected design is compared with the original prototype over 49 conditions per circuit. Four calibration policies separate nominal code transfer, local long-deadline calibration and deadline-aware bracketing. At a 1 ns deadline with absolute differential input at least 1 mV, the same local calibration policy changes passing grid coverage from 79.1% to 94.9% (310/392 versus 372/392 points). Excluding selection conditions gives 80.2% versus 94.8%. Matched mean core-rail energy is 143.01 versus 251.53 fJ/cycle, with a 1.90x gate-area proxy. These costs and unsuccessful cases are retained. Numerical refinement and input-driver/history/common-mode/load stresses challenge the declared comparison. The initial refinement found 14 failed deadline checks; 7 physical points were refined further and their corrected results, including worse outcomes, replace every matching reported policy row. The original coarse results remain available. The contribution is an auditable educational experiment around established circuitry, not a new topology or a silicon-performance claim. Width perturbations are deterministic stress, not foundry mismatch statistics; core-rail energy excludes external drivers and the calibration controller.
