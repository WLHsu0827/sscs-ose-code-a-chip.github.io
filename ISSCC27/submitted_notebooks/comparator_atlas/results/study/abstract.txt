Comparator Atlas: When Calibration Is Not Enough
Wei-Lun Hsu - National Tsing Hua University

Comparator Atlas is an open-source, notebook-driven study of when a regenerative comparator produces a correct decision before a deadline. It connects a declared nine-candidate SKY130 design search, a lower-energy comparison, numerical refinement and a real physical-layout flow. On the 49-condition schematic grid at 1 ns with sampled absolute input at least 1 mV, the same local calibration policy gives 310/392 original versus 372/392 selected correct points, at 143.01 and 251.53 fJ core energy. Actual GDS, DRC/LVS negative controls and distributed RC extraction connect that schematic study to a physical implementation. A separately declared full-grid study evaluates the nominal, code-zero schematic and RC circuits over 45 PVT conditions and four signed inputs each. All 180 RC samples meet the 2 ns primary deadline; 156 meet 1 ns, with the rest unresolved. The worst RC decision is 1.835 ns at FS/1.62 V/-40 C/-3 mV. Both modes use ngspice 47 with all 360 pointwise 10/5 ps comparisons confirmed. Mean full-grid core energy is 245.84 fJ schematic versus 425.49 fJ RC, exposing the parasitic cost rather than only reporting passing decisions. The earlier failed 1 ns pilot remains recorded separately. Source-bound tables, retained waveforms, vector figures and an interactive decision explorer support inspection and reuse. Results are deterministic sampled simulations, not silicon measurements, foundry yield or a continuous input-range guarantee; core energy excludes external drivers and calibration infrastructure.
